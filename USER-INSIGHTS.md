# Who we're talking to — and what's really stopping them

*User-insight brief behind the PS1 playable ad · © 2026 Chandra Prakash · prototype built for steer*

## The one-line insight

> **They don't lack information. They lack permission and trust.**
> They've tried for years, they've been told it's their fault, and now they're stuck between two loud camps: "injection lo, magic hai" and "ye cheating hai, dangerous hai". Nobody they trust has explained it **for them**.

An ad that only teaches facts loses them, because facts aren't the blocker. The blockers are **shame, fatigue, fear of failing again, and fear of being judged**. The ad has to deal with those first. After that, facts can land.

---

## 1. What the research says

### a) They are tired, and they've been made to feel it's their fault
- **Indians living with obesity report among the highest stigma of 14 countries** (Ipsos, Dec 2025–Jan 2026, India n=1,000):
  - 50% often feel self-conscious.
  - 49% often feel seen as lazy.
  - 43% feel seen as lacking willpower.
  - 71% feel anxious about their health (global average: 42%).
  - 82% have avoided social, leisure or romantic activities.
- **They blame themselves and still half-believe biology.** 75% say diet and exercise alone can fix obesity, the joint-highest of 14 countries. Yet 68% also say genetics and biology are the main cause. Ipsos calls this cognitive dissonance. It means they're **ready for the biology explanation**, but they've never been given permission to believe it.
- **They know the danger less than they should.** Only 37% link obesity to type 2 diabetes (global average: 53%).
- **Why people avoid doctors** (globally, Ipsos): they'd rather manage it themselves (31%), they fear failure (24%), they fear judgment (17%). The ACTION study (US, 2017) found that **82% believe losing weight is entirely their own responsibility**.
- **The body pushes back after weight loss.** Hunger hormones were still raised a year later (Sumithran, *NEJM* 2011). Their "failure" was biology. That's the reframe that unlocks them.

### b) GLP-1 in India right now: hype, judgment and confusion all at once
- **Hype:** "Mounjaro brides." Over 20% of one bariatric surgeon's injection enquiries come from women about to marry. Clinics sell pre-wedding packages. One woman: *"I've had men and their families reject my proposal because of my weight."* (Reuters, 2025–26)
- **Judgment:** two-thirds of GLP-1 users hide their treatment (70% of women). Of those who were judged, ~80% were told they were "taking the easy way out" (UK, Simple Online Pharmacy, 2026). Indian families add their own: *"bas ek roti kam khao."*
- **Sceptical voices with authority:** Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw (June 2026): *"For those who are not diabetic or clinically obese, diet and exercise should be the first option… not much long-term data on chronic use."*
- **Confusion and a trust gap:** semaglutide's patent expired in March 2026, and **50+ generic brands** are racing in, from about ₹3,400/month, against ₹17,000–26,000 for originator drugs. A doctor warns: *"if it's suspiciously low, it could be a red flag."* Cost is now less of a barrier. **Trust and supervision are the new barriers.**
- **People drop off:** Indian generic makers have reportedly cut sales targets by 25–30%. Reasons given: weekly injections, weight returning after stopping, and cautious doctors (Business Today, June 2026). Most "Mounjaro brides" stopped soon after the wedding.
- **What social media teaches them:** in the top 100 #Mounjaro TikToks, 95% of creators had no credentials, only 2% cited evidence, and 40% pushed discount codes (Kerner & Prescott, 2026). Brand line: *"Before you start, hear it from a doctor, not a reel."*

### c) Indian specifics that change the answer
- **Thin-fat bodies:** obesity is flagged from BMI 23. Waist size matters: 90 cm for men, 80 cm for women (Misra et al., 2025). About 4 in 10 Indian adults have abdominal obesity (ICMR-INDIAB, 2023).
- **Protein gap:** 60% of urban Indians don't regularly eat protein-rich food, and 74% don't know how much they need (LocalCircles, n≈207k, 2026). That directly worsens the **muscle-loss worry**.
- **PCOS:** 7–20% of Indian women aged 18–40 have it. Among them, 43% are obese by Asian cut-offs (Ganie et al., *JAMA Netw Open* 2024). This is a huge, under-served group asking "is it for me?"
- **Food is love:** "thoda aur kha lo", weddings, festivals, family dinners. Advice that ignores this feels foreign.

## 2. Their worries, ranked by how much they block action

| # | The worry (in their words) | What's underneath | What must be true for them to move |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Band kiya toh sab wapas aa jayega?"** | They've regained before. A failure that cost money too would be worse. | An honest yes, *plus* a plan for the exit: habits, muscle, and follow-up. |
| 2 | **"Zindagi bhar injection lena padega?"** | Fear of dependence, and of a "forever" cost | Honest: it's long-term care like BP medicine for some people, and the plan is personal. |
| 3 | **"Safe hai? Side effects?"** | WhatsApp-forward horror stories; now also unknown cheap brands | Real numbers, what's common and what's rare, who should *not* take it, and supervision. |
| 4 | **"Muscle chala jayega, kamzori aayegi?"** | "Ozempic face", looking ill, the low-protein Indian diet | Yes, it's a real risk and it's manageable: protein, strength training, body-composition tracking. |
| 5 | **"Ye shortcut / cheating hai na?"** | Shame and family judgment, and a reason to hide it | A reframe: it treats biology, and lifestyle still does half the work. Privacy respected. |
| 6 | **"Kya ye mere liye hai bhi?"** | "I'm not *that* fat", "only diabetics take it", PCOS confusion | A personal, honest check that can also say "you may not need it." |
| 7 | **"Sasta wala chemist se le lun?"** | Price-led self-medication in the generics era | Why screening and supervision matter, without fear-mongering. |

## 3. Psychology we design for

1. **Mirror before message.** People only listen once they feel seen. Open with *their* story (diets tried, weight back), not with our facts.
2. **Relief before education.** "It's not just willpower" lowers shame and defensiveness. Everything after that lands better.
3. **Two-sided honesty builds trust with sceptics.** Saying the uncomfortable thing (weight returns if you stop; it's not for everyone) is what makes the rest believable.
4. **Let them feel the change.** Ask how worried they are *before* and *after*. Seeing their own worry drop is the value, and it's measurable.
5. **Personal, not generic.** "What would a doctor look at for someone like *you*" beats any list of facts. It also answers the #6 worry.
6. **Privacy is a feature.** Two-thirds hide treatment. So: no login, nothing leaves the phone until they choose, a private WhatsApp chat, and the right to hear "not for you."
7. **Small, safe next step.** Not "book a consult". Instead: "send this to a doctor, ask your questions, no obligation." Low commitment beats high pressure.
8. **Indian, not translated.** Hinglish, rotis, chai, weddings, aunties, PCOS, a jeans size you already know.
